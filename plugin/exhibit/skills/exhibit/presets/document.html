Quarterly report

Support response times, Q3 2026

First responses got 38% faster after the triage change, but weekend tickets still wait more than twice as long.

  • Prepared by Customer Operations
  • Date 9 November 2026
  • Status Final
Summary
  • Median first response fell from 4.2 to 2.6 hours after triage moved to the shared queue in July.
  • Weekend tickets wait a median 6.1 hours, against 2.6 on weekdays.
  • Satisfaction rose to 91%, the highest this year, with no change in headcount.
  • We recommend one rotating weekend shift, starting in December.

Contents

    Background

    Until June, each product team triaged its own tickets. Tickets that touched more than one product were often passed between queues before anyone answered, which drove most of the long waits we reported in Q2.

    In July we moved triage to one shared queue, staffed in two-hour blocks by the existing team. This report compares July to September with the same months last year.

    Results

    Response times

    Every month of the quarter beat the same month last year, and the gap widened as the team got used to the new queue.

    By channel and day

    The weekend gap

    Weekend tickets are only 14% of volume but account for 41% of tickets that waited more than a day. Nobody is scheduled on Saturdays; Sunday cover is voluntary and uneven.

    Risk

    The December sale lands on a weekend. On last year's numbers, about 900 tickets would arrive with no one on shift.

    Recommendation

    Add one rotating weekend shift, four hours each day, starting 5 December. With twelve people on the rota, each person works one weekend shift every six weeks, paid as overtime.

    1. Publish the rota by 20 November.
    2. Route weekend tickets to the shared queue with a four-hour target.
    3. Review the numbers at the end of January.

    Sources

    1. Helpdesk export, 1 July to 30 September 2025 and 2026, all closed tickets.
    2. Post-ticket survey, 3,904 responses (26% response rate).
